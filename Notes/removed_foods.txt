Wild Game & Uncommon Meats
These meats are usually sourced from specialty butchers or via hunting and are rarely seen in a typical supermarket:
Large Game: Bear, Moose, Caribou, Wild Pig.
Small Game: Squirrel, Rabbit (including Stewed Rabbit), Pheasant, Quail.
Venison/Deer: Venison steaks, Venison sausage, Venison/deer jerky, and various prepared dishes (e.g., Venison with gravy or noodles).
Large Birds: Ostrich, Wild Goose (and Goose eggs).

Exotic Seafood
While some high-end markets may carry these, they are generally considered specialty items:
Abalone: A rare and expensive mollusk.
Shark & Eel: Including Shark steaks, Eel (often found in sushi like Unagi), and Eel sushi rolls.
Octopus & Squid: Including Octopus salad and Rice with squid.
Others: Frog legs, Turtle, and Caviar (sturgeon roe).

Unusual Organs & Specialty Parts
These parts of the animal are "variety meats" and are less common in modern open-aisle grocery displays:
Organs: Brains, Sweetbreads (thymus/pancreas), Tripe (stomach lining), and Tongue.
Specialties: Chitterlings (pig intestines), Gizzards, and Escargot (snails).
Prepared Dishes: Tongue pot roast and Stewed tripe.

Exotic Plants & Fruits
These items are typically found in international or specialty ethnic markets:
Cactus (Nopales): Raw or cooked cactus.
Tropical/Specialty Fruits: Lychee and Breadfruit.
Roots & Pastries: Lotus root and pastries made with lotus seed paste.
This list excludes more common items like liver, kidneys, or hearts, which are occasionally available in standard meat departments, focusing instead on items that truly fall into the "exotic" or "hard-to-find" category.
